# Identifying Risk Factors of 10-Year Stroke in Framingham Heart Study 

This project seeks to identify key risk factors affecting the likelihood of a stroke. Data is sourced from the Framingham Heart Study, a long-term longitudinal investigation that followed the health outcomes of 5,209 participants with 24 years of follow-up from 1948 onward. Data was collected during three examination periods occurring approximately six years apart from 1956 to 1968; the initial analysis cohort for this project includes 4,434 participants with adjudicated laboratory, clinical, and questionnaire measurements across these time points. Armed with strong, reliable longitudinal data, this project aims to calculate the ten-year probability of stroke across different levels of patient risk to better understand the effects of key baseline clinical characteristics (age, diabetes, and systolic blood pressure, and others). Due to known differences in stroke outcomes and risk factors, all analyses will be stratified by sex. Additionally, this project will seek to determine whether a more advanced, time-varying survival analysis is necessary to effectively capture changes in covariates over time.
