# Evaluation of Treatment Efficacy Among Hard-Drug Using HIV Patients Receiving Highly Active Antiretroviral Therapy (HAART)

This project attempts to understand how the two-year response to highly active antiretroviral therapy (HAART) for HIV differs for patients who use hard drugs like cocaine and heroin relative to non-hard drug users. Data was sourced from the Multicenter AIDS Cohort Study, which records treatment histories of HIV-infected men in four major American cities. The efficacy of the HAART treatment can be quantified through laboratory measures, such as viral load (number of HIV copies in 1 mL of blood) and CD4+ T cell count as well as participant-reported quality of life scores from the Short Form Health Survey (SF-36 physical and mental components, PCS and MCS). Thus, we sought to determine whether there is a significant association between hard drug use at baseline and the 2-year change in each of the four outcomes (viral load, CD4+ T cell count, SF-36 physical, SF-36 mental) when controlling for standard covariates (age, education status, smoking status, BMI, and race). Additionally, we tested to see if these associations are meaningfully changed when controlling for adherence to HAART.
