Evaluation of Saliva Procurement and Integrated Testing (SPIT) booklet in Measuring Cortisol and DHEA Levels
==
Cortisol and DHEA are adrenal hormones that affect stress levels in the body; measuring diurnal trends in these hormones is a key facet of endocrinologic research. As such, developing effective modes of sample collection is critical to obtaining reliable estimates of hormonal variations. This report seeks to evaluate the efficacy of the Saliva Procurement and Integrated Testing (SPIT) booklet and analyze trends in cortisol and DHEA variation. Data for this project was collected from 31 healthy control subjects four times a day in the following sequence: at wake, 30 minutes after wake, lunchtime (variable by participant), and 600 minutes after wake. This process was repeated for three consecutive days, with the time of measurement being recorded both by an electronic monitoring cap (cap time), which houses the SPIT booklet throughout the day, and manually by participants in the booklet (booklet time). To evaluate efficacy, internal consistency of timing (if on average, cap and booklet times were similar) and treatment adherence (the proportion of participants completing measurements on time) were analyzed. Also, this project seeks to determine whether there is an association between time since wake and cortisol and DHEA levels and whether this association follows the established diurnal trends.
